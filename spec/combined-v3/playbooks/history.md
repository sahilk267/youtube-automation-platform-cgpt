# History OS — SOURCE PROVENANCE & CHRONOLOGICAL RECONSTRUCTION

1. Collect primary document metadata, origin, archive accession, image/scan rights and authenticity status; secondary historians separately tagged.
2. Normalize dates and calendars including uncertainty windows and geographical name changes; record translation languages and edition.
3. Construct chronology of events with first-hand documentary anchors and known disputes; distinguish popular myths from attested facts.
4. Evaluate historiographical interpretations with scholars' evidence and historical context, no modern assumption inserted without qualification.
5. Draw maps/visual reenactments with period-appropriate labels and transparent speculation flags; no synthetic illustration passed off as archive photo.
6. Output: dated evidence map, citations, disagreements, translation notes, reconstruction disclosure and review flags.
